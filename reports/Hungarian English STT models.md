# Test Canary, but fix the pipeline first

No published evidence shows any self-hostable model beating your current setup on mixed Hungarian-English commands, because **no source evaluates Hungarian-English code-switching or suffixed English names at all**. Your measured baseline (sarpba turbo q5_0, beam 5: 23.1% WER, 526 ms median, 20/24 commands) therefore stands until a bake-off says otherwise. The one new model worth testing first is **Canary 1B v2 on transcribe.cpp Vulkan**, the only candidate that combines confirmed Hungarian, a better FLEURS score and an estimated latency well inside budget. Parakeet is the warning: its FLEURS score nearly ties your current model, yet it scored 52.9% against 24.0% on your voice, so FLEURS rankings are weak predictors here. The largest gains are likely to come from outside the acoustic model, through a two-language `<|hu|><|en|>` prompt test and catalog-and-verb matching, because your assistant needs the right command rather than a perfect transcript. Your 24 recordings can eliminate candidates but cannot prove a winner: with only four failed commands to win back, even a perfect result falls short of statistical significance. Every latency figure for a new model below is an extrapolation, since nothing has been measured on an RX 6700 or other Navi 22 card.

Same-model spread on one dataset reaches about 2 WER points: Whisper-large-v3 on FLEURS Hungarian scores 14.11 in NVIDIA's table ([NVIDIA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14128)), 14.62 normalized in sarpba's ([sarpba](https://huggingface.co/sarpba/whisper-teszt-eredmenyek)) and 16.32 in Speechmatics' ([Speechmatics](https://www.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/hungarian)). Treat smaller cross-source gaps as noise.

The most useful same-pipeline comparison is transcribe.cpp's own Q8_0 FLEURS order: Canary 13.06, base turbo 15.07, Parakeet 16.06, Qwen 1.7B 32.84, Qwen 0.6B 56.24 ([Canary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/canary-1b-v2.md), [Parakeet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3.md)). It covers only base turbo, not your fine-tune. Your fine-tune is also not clearly ahead of vanilla models on FLEURS: 16.26 against 14.62 for large-v3 and 16.05 for base turbo ([sarpba](https://huggingface.co/sarpba/whisper-teszt-eredmenyek)), while winning clearly on Common Voice (10.59 against 19.45 for base turbo).

The Parakeet result should discount all of this. A FLEURS gap of 0.5 points predicted a near tie; your recordings showed a 29-point gap. FLEURS is read speech, your clips are short commands with embedded foreign names, and part of the gap could come from the int8 ONNX pipeline. No published figure covers short voice-assistant commands on any Hungarian model. I therefore read Canary's 3.4-point FLEURS lead as a reason to test it, and not as a forecast. Canary comes from the same vendor evaluation and has not been shown to handle embedded names better.

## Code-switching evidence points away from long-sentence benchmarks

The closest multi-model study, ServiceNow's June 2026 benchmark, covers Spanish, French, Canadian French and German with English, and its design is close to the reverse of your task. Its audio is synthetic, utterances run 12-40 words, entity and product names do not count as switchable words, and entity-dominated utterances are excluded ([ServiceNow](https://huggingface.co/blog/ServiceNow-AI/code-switching)). Its useful signal is that Whisper turbo's errors concentrated in the embedded English, with WER ranging from 0.16 to 0.61 across conditions ([ServiceNow](https://huggingface.co/blog/ServiceNow-AI/code-switching)). Parakeet v3's card claims language auto-detection and says nothing about switching inside a sentence ([NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)), so "multilingual" should not be read as "code-switching".

The best transferable result is from Mandarin-English. Concatenating both language tokens in Whisper's decoder prompt reduced total error compared with the default on all three test sets: 20.9 against 22.8, 32.7 against 38.2, and 47.6 against 65.0 ([Peng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.11095)). On validation data, forcing Mandarin pushed English WER to 93-97 ([Peng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.11095)). Hungarian shares a script with English, so the failure should be milder and more phonetic, but nobody has measured it. The experiment costs almost nothing in latency, adding one prompt token. Whether whisper.cpp exposes this without a patch was not verified, and this project's forced-versus-auto setting is not stated in the baselines, so record it in the test.

Your own result, where an initial prompt listing names raised WER to 31.7-45%, matches the literature. Prompting works when gated: KWS-Whisper spots entities first and prompts only those, raising entity recall from 87.3% to 97.7% and from 79.9% to 96.9% on internal Mandarin-English sets ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09552v4)). A long static prompt is a different method and should stay retired.

## Your live failures are partly not a code-switching problem

"Állítsd meg" misheard as "Árisd meg" is a plain Hungarian verb error, and no entity method addresses it. Of the 24 recorded phrases, only **seven contain an English name** (clips 08, 09, 11, 15, 16, 17, 18), and five of those carry a Hungarian suffix on the name (Shreket, Gumpot, Jude-ot, Attacket, Attacktől). The rest of the set exercises Hungarian carrier sentences and device commands. A model chosen only for English-name handling could therefore lose on the commands you use most. "Stop" and its neighbours belong in a small closed verb grammar, matched phonetically after transcription, whichever model wins. The four "Árisd meg" events exceed the three failures in the 13/16 live count, so some occurred outside that count or still executed; the per-event log is worth checking before building on it.

For names, retrieval from a known catalog has the strongest evidence closest to your domain. Apple's music-query study, apparently in English, cut WER by 32.6-39.0% on synthetic sets and 7.7% on STOP using a 2.6M-entity catalog, and its orthographic acoustic embeddings retrieved head entities at 84.8% top-1 recall against 53.5% for BM25 ([Apple](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06062v1)). RECOVER cut entity WER by 14.5-45.7% on English data with exact, fuzzy and phonetic candidates plus constrained LLM selection, but its main model is cloud GPT-4o and a weaker model raised WER on one dataset ([RECOVER](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16411)). Neither reports latency. My inference is that a home catalog of thousands of names should match in single-digit milliseconds without any LLM, and that a closed catalog cannot invent an artist, though it can still pick the wrong one. Your existing fuzzy title match already rescued one command at greedy, which is a small but real local signal. The design needs a suffix stripper and a dual key per name, one English and one Hungarian-reading ("Massive" and "Messzív"). No Hungarian phonetic-matching evaluation exists for this.

Decoder biasing has larger published numbers but a worse fit. TurboBias lifted Canary beam F-score from 53.7 to 82.4 on English at 2-5% runtime cost, while WER rose from 9.86% to 11.42% as lists grew to 20K phrases ([TurboBias](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014v2)). A trie on Whisper large-v3 cut biased-word WER from 9.86 to 5.66 on English, but needed TTS-derived variants and reported no latency ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17796v2)). None of these studies tests inflected foreign names, so "Attacktől" remains an open risk. TurboBias's GPU path also runs in NeMo, which has no easy AMD route here. Biasing should follow retrieval, and only if clips remain that retrieval cannot resolve.

## Vulkan estimates give Canary ample headroom, unmeasured

ggml Vulkan is the one low-friction AMD path. ONNX Runtime has no Vulkan provider, and its release notes (seen secondhand) dropped ROCm ([ORT docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/), [PR copy](https://github.com/jchunk-io/jchunk/pull/89)). That makes sherpa-onnx, onnx-asr and faster-whisper effectively CPU-only here. The enabling find is **transcribe.cpp**, an MIT-licensed ggml runtime at a young 0.2 release with Vulkan builds of Canary, Parakeet and Qwen3-ASR ([README](https://github.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp)).

The estimates below scale published 4750U iGPU timings to a 5 s clip, then divide by an assumed 2.5-8x RX 6700 speedup. The low end derives from turbo's timings and assumes full encoder context; the high end is a judgment, not a source. They omit fixed overhead and host-side time on the i7-6700, so they run optimistic. Canary still fits even if its high estimate doubles, which leaves headroom for beam search and catalog matching.

| Model | Published 4750U Vulkan, 11 s clip | Estimated RX 6700, 5 s clip | i7-6700 CPU estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parakeet v3 Q8_0 | 453 ms | ~25-80 ms | 203 ms measured (int8 ONNX) |
| Canary 1B v2 Q8_0 | 759 ms | ~40-145 ms | ~320-900 ms, sources disagree |
| Qwen3 1.7B Q4_K_M | 2.02 s | ~115-450 ms | ~960-1120 ms |
| Current turbo | 4.40 s (base turbo Q8_0) | 451 ms greedy, 526 ms beam 5, measured | ~2.7 s, not viable |

Timings are from the transcribe.cpp docs for [Parakeet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3.md), [Canary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/canary-1b-v2.md), [Qwen3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/qwen3-asr-1.7b.md) and [turbo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/handy-computer/transcribe.cpp/main/docs/models/whisper-large-v3-turbo.md).

Your current beam 5 setup already exceeds the budget at its 656 ms maximum. Lowering audio_ctx from 400 to 300 still covers 6 s and might save about 100 ms if cost scales linearly, an estimate with no published accuracy data behind it. Large-v3 fine-tunes have no latency figure at all, and at about 2.5 times turbo's parameters they should count as over budget until measured. VRAM use on transcribe.cpp is also unpublished, with only file sizes available (Canary Q8_0 1.14 GB), so measure it alongside your LLM.

## Ranked test plan for the 24 recordings

The order weighs expected gain, evidence quality, and effort. Run everything through one normalizer, and score parser command success first, then named-entity resolution on the seven English-name clips, then CER and WER, then median and maximum latency. A Hungarian card notes that agglutination makes WER count one wrong suffix as a whole-word error ([BuzzASR](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/hungarian)), which makes CER a useful companion here. `bench.py` was not reviewed for this report.

| Rank | Test | Why | Evidence | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current turbo plus verb grammar and suffix-aware phonetic catalog match, replayed on saved transcripts | Hits "Árisd meg" and suffixed names; no model change or latency risk | Moderate for catalogs (English music domain); **none in Hungarian** | Low |
| 2 | Canary 1B v2 Q8_0, transcribe.cpp Vulkan | Best reachable FLEURS score and ample latency headroom | **Thin**: one dataset, vendor-run, no Vulkan timing on your card | Low-moderate |
| 3 | Current turbo with forced `hu`, auto-detect and `<|hu|><|en|>` | Near-zero latency cost; helped on Mandarin-English | **Analogy only**; possible whisper.cpp patch | Low-moderate |
| 4 | Parakeet v3 Q8_0, transcribe.cpp Vulkan, as a diagnostic | Shows whether 52.9% came from int8 ONNX or from the model, which recalibrates your trust in Canary's score | Direct measurement | Very low once rank 2 is built |
| 5 | benmajor27 large-v3 fine-tune, converted to ggml | Tests how much Hungarian accuracy costs in latency; ceiling for the Whisper path | Moderate on accuracy, author-run; **latency unknown** | Moderate |
| 6 | Current turbo at audio_ctx 300, beam 5 | Could bring the 656 ms maximum into budget | Estimate only | Very low |
| 7 | BuzzASR/hungarian, converted to ggml | Same class as rank 5; run only if rank 5 shows promise | Moderate; same latency unknown | Moderate |
| 8 | Qwen3-ASR 1.7B Q4_K_M | One-off check that the poor scores are real, since the runtime is already installed | Scores weak, source conflict open | Very low |

Rank 1 replays existing transcripts, so it takes no fresh inference for the baseline, and it should also be applied to every later candidate, since post-correction can change the ranking. Hotword biasing is deliberately excluded for now: sherpa-onnx supports it only with transducers in `modified_beam_search` ([sherpa](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/hotwords/index.html)), its fit with the Parakeet v3 export is unconfirmed, and hotwords would not repair Parakeet's Hungarian carrier-sentence errors. Personal LoRA fine-tuning also waits. Reported gains are about 10-25% relative WER from group or anecdotal results ([PI-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15668)), no study covers one speaker with 50-500 sentences, and training on these 24 clips would leave nothing honest to test on.

Several candidates can be dropped now. Granite Speech, Moonshine and the released SenseVoiceSmall lack Hungarian ([Granite](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.0-1b-speech), [Moonshine](https://moonshine-voice.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/available-models/), [SenseVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/SenseVoice)), and Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime's 13 languages exclude it ([Mistral](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602)). Omnilingual is PyTorch-only and the smallest variant needs about 5 GiB of VRAM ([Meta](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-3B/blob/main/README.md)). SeamlessM4T has no port and a non-commercial licence ([Meta](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large/blob/16579d41e2fd5ca128e348506767162142666271/README.md)), and Wav2vec2 Hungarian sits near 31% WER ([card](https://huggingface.co/jonatasgrosman/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-hungarian)). Cohere Transcribe scored 133.65 raw WER on Hungarian ([BuzzASR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09554)). Licences block none of the ranked candidates for private home use.

## Twenty-four clips can reject models but not crown one

The phrase file holds **104 words**, so 24.0% and 23.1% WER are 25 and 24 word errors: greedy and beam 5 differ by a single word, and each command is worth 4.2 points. The extra parser success from beam 5 is one clip. Since 20/24 leaves four failures, a candidate that fixes all four with no new failures scores 4-0 on discordant pairs, an exact two-sided McNemar p of 0.125. **No outcome on this set reaches p < 0.05 for command success against beam 5.** The baseline settings were also tuned on these same clips, which flatters them slightly, and all clips come from one speaker, with the live log's 13/16 (81%) the only out-of-set check.

The practical rule, and these thresholds are my judgment, is to use the 24 clips as a screen. Drop any candidate that scores below 18/24, resolves fewer than five of the seven English-name clips, or exceeds 600 ms on a typical clip. Advance survivors to a new set of about 60-100 clips, recorded in real room conditions, weighted towards the stop verb and suffixed names, and including names absent from every prompt, catalog tuning and training run. Replace the current model only on the new set, and only if command success beats it with no regression on the frequent device commands.

The thinnest evidence sits at the centre of your question. Nothing measures Hungarian-English switching, entity accuracy, or suffixed foreign names on any model, and short-command Hungarian accuracy is unpublished too. Latency on your card is unmeasured for all new candidates, and short-clip overhead is unknown. Canary's language-hint behaviour on mixed input was not examined. The Qwen3 Hungarian scores conflict (32.84, 43.02 raw, and an unresolved aggregate), and the sarpba table is author-run with an undocumented normalizer. Omnilingual Hungarian, flagged unconfirmed in one research thread, is confirmed by Meta's per-language table listing `hun_Latn` ([CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr/main/per_language_results_table_7B_llm_asr.csv)), although that does not rescue its runtime.

## Conclusion

The question assumed a better model might exist; the evidence suggests the better question is where the remaining failures live. With four failed commands in 24 and at least one recurring failure being an ordinary Hungarian verb, a closed-vocabulary command layer can plausibly recover as much as a model swap, at no latency cost and with less operational risk than adopting a 0.2-release runtime. The Parakeet gap is the key calibration: a public benchmark near-tie became a 29-point loss on your voice, so this project's own recordings outrank any published table.

The bigger asset to build is a larger test set, not another candidate list. It is the only way to separate real gains from one-clip noise, and it would make every later choice, including Canary, biasing or a personal LoRA, cheap to decide. If Canary survives the screen, its latency headroom is the prize: the 300-400 ms it might free could fund beam search and catalog matching, which the Whisper path cannot afford.
